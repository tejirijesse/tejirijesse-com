## Context

I did not start Justly and I do not run it. Linda is the CEO and the public
voice; Ali is the CTO. I invested and I advise, across Nigeria and Uganda, from
2025.

It is on this site because the reason I invested is the same argument as
everything else here — a person's rights are only real if there is a record
they can produce — and because being on the outside of a project taught me
something about my own defaults that founding four companies did not.

## Problem

A person's rights are only real if there is a record they can produce, and the
moment a person most needs that record is the moment they are least able to
pay a lawyer to make one.

## Consequences

An AI that explains your rights is a system that speaks with authority to a
person in distress. Every property that makes it useful — immediacy,
confidence, availability at two in the morning — is also what makes an
incomplete answer dangerous. Someone acts on it. In a police station.

And the evidence vault has a symmetry problem I have not seen resolved
anywhere. A record of what happened protects you from the account of a more
powerful party. The same record, subpoenaed or seized or leaked, is a complete
account of your movements and contacts handed to that same party. Encryption
changes who can read it. It does not change that it exists.

## What I still cannot answer

A frightened person acting on an incomplete summary of their rights may be
worse off than one who knew they had no information at all. I do not know how
to build a legal tool that communicates its own uncertainty to someone who
urgently needs it not to be uncertain.
