## Context

Roughly fifty million African youth athletes compete each year and under one
percent leave any trace behind. Rovify started in Kigali in 2024 as a record
system for that population, and on 4 April 2026 it ran Rwanda's first fully
digitised youth tournament, the RYA Launch Cup, at Mpano Recreation Center.

## Problem

A child can be the best player in a district and leave no evidence of it.

Stated before any technology: careers in this system are decided by who
happened to be watching. Everything downstream — selection, scholarships,
signings — runs on the memory of adults who were present, and memory is not
distributed evenly across districts, schools or families.

## People

Players, most of them minors. Parents. Coaches, who are usually the only
record-keepers. Federation officials, who must certify results they did not
witness. And scouts, who act on claims they cannot check.

## What I believed

We believed the problem was visibility — that African players were not seen by
the people who could sign them, and that a platform which made performance
legible to scouts would move careers.

## Outcome

One fully digitised national tournament, run on 4 April 2026. Pricing
established at $500 a year for schools and $2,500 and up for federations.
Onboarding underway, revenue not yet material.

The claim I can defend is that it works operationally. The claim I cannot yet
defend is that institutions will keep paying for it once the novelty of the
first tournament has passed.

## Consequences

This is the project where the consequence is sharpest, because the subjects are
children and the record is designed to be permanent.

A verified athletic record is an asset for a player who develops on schedule. For
a player who does not, it is a durable account of not being good enough, created
before they could consent to it and attached to them at an age when almost
everyone's trajectory is noise. Scouting is already a filtering system with a
strong bias toward early physical maturity; a searchable record makes that
filter cheaper to apply and harder to escape.

There is a second-order version that concerns me more. Once federations hold
verified records, records become a condition of participation, and the child
without documentation is excluded from the competition rather than merely
undocumented within it. The system built to include them becomes the gate.

## What I still cannot answer

A permanent record does not distinguish between the season a fifteen-year-old
was injured and the season he was not good enough. I built a system that
remembers, for children, and I have not resolved what a person should be able
to make it forget.
